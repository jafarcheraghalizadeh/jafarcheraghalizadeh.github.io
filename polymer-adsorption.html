Explore the adsorption model

Change the surface attraction \(\epsilon\) and temperature to see how a chain attaches to the substrate. Run the simulation or advance it in steps; live values show contacts, energy, chain size, and sample averages.

□ Polymer● Surface contact◇ Fixed end

Squares represent \(2\times2\) occupied lattice sites. Dots mark surface contacts; a diamond marks the fixed first monomer. The view automatically fits the chain. One Monte Carlo sweep is \(N\) attempted local moves; speed controls playback, not physical time.

Model and sampling

The chain lives on a square lattice above a hard substrate at \(y=0\). Each monomer occupies \(2\times2\) lattice sites. A contacting monomer contributes \(-\epsilon\). With lower-edge heights \(y_i\), the contact count and energy are

\[n_{\mathrm{contact}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}_{\{y_i=0\}}.\]
\[E=-\epsilon\,n_{\mathrm{contact}}.\]

The indicator is one for a surface contact and zero otherwise. Monomers cannot overlap. There is no additional monomer–monomer attraction or bond energy.

For monomer centers \(\mathbf r_i\), allowed bond lengths in units of the lattice spacing \(a\) are

\[\frac{\lVert\mathbf r_{i+1}-\mathbf r_i\rVert}{a}\in\left\{2,\sqrt{5},\sqrt{8},3,\sqrt{10},\sqrt{13}\right\}.\]

Each proposal moves one randomly selected monomer by one lattice spacing along a coordinate axis. Invalid lengths, overlap, substrate penetration, and bond crossings are rejected. Chain initialization also rejects intersections, and movement checks cover the area swept by a bond.

For a geometrically valid proposal, the energy change and Metropolis acceptance probability are

\[\Delta E=E_{\mathrm{new}}-E_{\mathrm{old}}=-\epsilon\,\Delta n_{\mathrm{contact}}.\]
\[P_{\mathrm{acc}}=\min\!\left\{1,\exp\!\left(-\frac{\Delta E}{k_{\mathrm B}T}\right)\right\}.\]

The energy scale \(E_0\) is arbitrary. The controls use reduced attraction and temperature:

\[\epsilon^{*}=\frac{\epsilon}{E_0},\qquad T^{*}=\frac{k_{\mathrm B}T}{E_0}.\]

Their ratio determines the energetic bias toward adsorption:

\[\frac{\epsilon^{*}}{T^{*}}=\frac{\epsilon}{k_{\mathrm B}T}.\]

The first monomer is fixed at the substrate by default. Turn off tethering to study a free chain; in this unbounded half-plane it can diffuse away from the surface. The controls use \(N\) monomers and \(N-1\) bonds.

Changing \(\epsilon\), temperature, or the discard interval clears statistics and begins a new warm-up window from the current configuration. Changing \(N\), seed, or tethering, or restarting, constructs a new chain and clears statistics. Speed preserves the trajectory and samples.

Measured observables

The contact fraction is the portion of the chain touching the surface:

\[f_{\mathrm{contact}}=\frac{n_{\mathrm{contact}}}{N}.\]

The radius of gyration \(R_g\) measures the chain’s size about its center of mass:

\[\mathbf r_{\mathrm{cm}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf r_i.\]
\[R_g=\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lVert\mathbf r_i-\mathbf r_{\mathrm{cm}}\rVert^2}.\]

Post-warm-up means use one sample per sweep. For \(M\) retained samples of an observable \(A\), the displayed sample mean is

\[\overline{A}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}A_m.\]

The mean chain-size readout averages \(R_g\), rather than taking the square root of the mean of \(R_g^2\). Center height is the vertical coordinate of \(\mathbf r_{\mathrm{cm}}\). Samples are correlated; the discard interval does not establish equilibrium. Repeat runs and compare longer warm-up periods before drawing quantitative conclusions.

YOUR TURN

Practice: compare attraction and temperature

  1. With tethering enabled and \(T^{*}=1\), compare \(\epsilon^{*}=0\) and \(\epsilon^{*}=3\). Let each run warm up and record its mean contact fraction.
  2. Compare \((\epsilon^{*},T^{*})=(1.5,1)\) with \((\epsilon^{*},T^{*})=(3,2)\). Use the same seed, chain length, and number of sweeps.
  3. Repeat with a longer discard interval and several seeds. Check how sensitive the averages are to these choices.
Show the expected behavior

Increasing \(\epsilon/(k_{\mathrm B}T)\) favors surface contacts. The two parameter pairs in the second task have the same ratio:

\[\frac{1.5}{1}=\frac{3}{2}=1.5.\]

This model therefore assigns them the same Metropolis acceptance probabilities. With identical initial conditions and random seed, they follow the same configuration trajectory; their energies in units of \(E_0\) differ by a factor of two. A free chain can desorb and diffuse away.
