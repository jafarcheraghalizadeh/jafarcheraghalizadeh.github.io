How the algorithm works

The square lattice has spins \(s_i=\pm1\), coupling \(J=1\), no external field, and periodic boundaries. Each nearest-neighbor bond is counted once:

\[E=-J\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}s_i s_j.\]

A proposed flip from \(E\) to \(E'\) is accepted with probability

\[P_{\mathrm{acc}}=\min\!\left(1,\frac{g(E)}{g(E')}\right).\]

After every attempt, including rejections, update the occupied energy:

\[\ln g(E)\leftarrow\ln g(E)+\ln f,\qquad H(E)\leftarrow H(E)+1.\]

Sampling begins with \(\ln g=0\) and \(\ln f=1\). Each flat-histogram stage halves \(\ln f\). Impossible energies \(E=\pm124J\) are excluded. Flatness is a heuristic, and a small modification factor does not guarantee an accurate estimate.

The density of states determines the partition function and canonical energy weights:

\[Z(T)=\sum_E g(E)e^{-\beta E},\qquad\beta=\frac{1}{k_BT}.\]
\[p(E,T)=\frac{g(E)e^{-\beta E}}{Z(T)}.\]

Energy and heat capacity per spin are

\[u=\frac{\langle E\rangle}{N},\qquad c_V=\frac{C_V}{N}=\frac{\langle E^2\rangle-\langle E\rangle^2}{Nk_BT^2}.\]

The two ground states fix the normalization of \(g(E)\), giving free energy and entropy per spin:

\[f=\frac{F}{N}=-\frac{k_BT}{N}\ln Z,\qquad s=\frac{S}{N}=\frac{u-f}{T}.\]

Magnetic observables combine canonical weights with measured conditional moments at each energy:

\[\langle |M|\rangle_T=\sum_E p(E,T)\langle |M|\rangle_E.\]

At zero field, symmetry gives \(\langle M\rangle=0\). Susceptibility per spin and the Binder cumulant are

\[\chi=\frac{\langle M^2\rangle}{Nk_BT},\qquad U_4=1-\frac{\langle M^4\rangle}{3\langle M^2\rangle^2}.\]

Magnetic estimates appear only after every accessible energy has measurement samples.

This is a teaching simulation. Estimates during adaptation are provisional, and production estimates use correlated samples without error bars. The displayed spin configuration samples the energy landscape, not the thermal ensemble at the slider temperature. Use independent runs and uncertainty analysis for research.

Practice: accept a proposed flip

Suppose \(\ln g(E)=12\) and \(\ln g(E\prime)=14\). What is the acceptance probability?

Show the worked answer

The probability is \(\min[1,e^{12-14}]=e^{-2}\approx0.1353\). Whether the flip succeeds or fails, update ln g and H at the energy occupied after the decision.
